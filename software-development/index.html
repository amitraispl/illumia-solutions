How We Work

A cycle, not a straight line.

Discovery doesn't end when design starts, and testing doesn't wait for the end. Every stage stays open to what the next one learns — your team reviews and weighs in throughout, not just at kickoff and handoff.

01

Discover

Stakeholder interviews, technical audits, and scope framing — revisited every sprint as real usage and new context reshape what we thought we knew.

02

Design

Wireframes and UX flows shaped with your team, sent back for another pass whenever testing or review surfaces something the first draft missed.

03

Prototype

Clickable prototypes validated with real users before production code is committed — and again, any time a later stage reveals a gap.

04

Develop

Iterative builds in short sprints, each one shippable to staging so scope can adapt before it hardens into something harder to change.

05

Test

QA folded into every sprint, not saved for the end — issues route straight back into design or development while they're still cheap to fix.

06

Review

Stakeholder walkthroughs each sprint surface what's working and what isn't, feeding directly into the next round of design and build.

07

Iterate

Insights from testing, review, and stakeholder feedback loop back into earlier phases — the cycle repeats until the product is right, not just shipped.

Iterate feeds straight back into Discover

Agile Delivery

Those seven stages repeat — they don't run once.

Discover, design, prototype, develop, test, review, and iterate aren't a straight line we walk once and hand off. We work in short sprints, so any stage can send us back to an earlier one — you see working software early and often, and scope adapts as testing, reviews, and stakeholder feedback teach us something the initial spec couldn't.

Short Sprints

Work broken into fixed, short cycles — usually two weeks — each ending in something you can actually review.

Ship Early, Ship Often

Staging environments updated every sprint, not unveiled at the end. You see the product take shape as it's built.

Backlog, Not a Blueprint

Priorities re-ordered as we learn — from user feedback, real data, or a shift in your business — without renegotiating the whole project.

Continuous Feedback

Regular reviews with your team keep design, engineering, and integration work aligned to what's actually needed, sprint over sprint.
